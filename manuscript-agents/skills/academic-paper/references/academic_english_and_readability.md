# Academic English & Readability

For clarity and for non-native English authors. Goal: precise, plain, reviewer-friendly prose.

## Sentence-level
- One idea per sentence; prefer 15-25 words. Break up >40-word sentences.
- **Active voice** for what you did ("We measured…"); passive is fine where the actor is irrelevant.
- Strong verbs over nominalizations ("we analyzed" not "we performed an analysis of").
- Cut hedging stacks ("it may possibly suggest" → "suggests"); cut filler ("it is important to note that").
- Consistent terminology — name one concept one way; don't elegant-variation a key term.

## Paragraph-level
- Topic sentence first; one point per paragraph; signpost transitions (However, Therefore, In contrast).
- Old-to-new flow: start sentences with known information, end with the new.

## Tense conventions
- Methods/Results: past ("participants completed…", "the effect was…").
- Established facts / what figures show: present ("Figure 2 shows…", "obesity increases risk").
- Prior literature: present-perfect or past ("studies have shown…").

## Non-native support
- Articles (a/an/the), prepositions, and count/noncount nouns are common slips — check key terms.
- Avoid idioms and phrasal-verb ambiguity in formal writing.
- Read aloud or use text-to-speech to catch awkward rhythm.

## Discipline, not deception
This improves clarity; it is not about hiding AI use. Disclose AI assistance per journal policy. Keep the author's voice (style calibration in `academic_writing_style.md`).
