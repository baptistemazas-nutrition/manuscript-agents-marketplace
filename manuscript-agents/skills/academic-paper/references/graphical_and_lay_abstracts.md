# Graphical & Lay Abstracts

Increasingly requested; widen reach and comprehension.

## Graphical abstract
- A single, self-contained visual of the study's core message (question → approach → key result).
- Simple, uncluttered, readable as a thumbnail; follow the journal's size/format spec.
- Honest: it must represent the actual finding and its scope, not an idealized claim.

## Lay / plain-language summary
- 1 short paragraph in non-specialist language (aim ~ grade 8-10 reading level): what question, what you found, why it matters.
- No jargon or undefined acronyms; no overstated implications; note the limits ("in this study", "associated with", not "causes" unless the design supports it).
- Useful for funders, patient communities, press, and social media.

## Audience care
For health topics, be accurate and non-alarmist; distinguish association from causation; avoid implying individual medical advice.
