# Reference Styles & Managers

Match the journal exactly; inconsistent references are a common desk-reject trigger.

## Major styles (know which the journal wants)
| Style | In-text | Used by |
|---|---|---|
| **APA 7** | (Author, Year) | psychology, education, social sciences |
| **Vancouver / ICMJE** | numbered [1] in order of appearance | most biomedical journals |
| **IEEE** | numbered [1] | engineering, CS |
| **Chicago** | notes-bibliography OR author-date | humanities, some sciences |
| **Harvard** | (Author Year) | many; journal-specific variants |
| **AMA** | superscript numbers | medical |
- Always verify against the specific journal's author guidelines — house variants are common.

## Reference managers (recommended workflow)
- **Zotero** (free/open), **Mendeley**, **EndNote**, or **BibTeX/BibLaTeX** (LaTeX).
- Collect with one-click capture + DOIs; let the manager format and **switch styles** in one step (CSL styles cover thousands of journals).
- For LaTeX: keep a `.bib`; cite with `\cite{}`; the journal's `.bst`/CSL controls formatting.
- Zotero is installed on this machine — its library can seed citations and reformat to the target style.

## Integrity (pairs with manuscript-citations)
- Verify every DOI resolves and the cited source actually supports the claim (source-claim coherence).
- Check for retractions (Retraction Watch) before citing.
- Don't pad with uncited-in-text references or coercive citations.
