# Title, Abstract & Keywords — discoverability

Most readers find a paper by title/abstract in a search engine. Optimize for both humans and indexing.

## Title
- Specific, informative, and front-loaded with the key terms a searcher would type.
- State the topic and, where appropriate, the design or the finding ("… : a randomized controlled trial", "… reduces …").
- Avoid jargon-only titles, unexplained acronyms, cute wordplay that hides content, and "Investigation into…".
- Many fields favor a clear declarative or descriptive title; keep within the journal's length limit.

## Abstract
- Mirror the paper; every number in it must match the text.
- Structured (Background/Methods/Results/Conclusions) if the journal requires; otherwise a tight ~200-300 word arc.
- Lead with the gap and the aim; report the primary result **with effect size + CI** (not just "significant"); end with the take-home and its scope.
- Self-contained: define essential abbreviations; no citations, no undefined references to figures.

## Keywords
- Use terms NOT already in the title (broaden coverage). Prefer the field's controlled vocabulary (e.g., **MeSH** for biomedicine) so indexers and searchers match you.
- Mix specific (your exact method/condition) and broader (the field) terms.

## Check
Would a researcher searching your topic find this by its title + keywords? If not, revise the terms.
