# Response to Reviewers (Rebuttal)

Revisions are won or lost here. Reviewers re-read the paper through your responses.

## Principles
1. **Answer every point** — number them exactly as the reviewers did; skip nothing, including minor ones.
2. **Be respectful and grateful**, even for harsh or wrong critiques. Tone matters to the editor.
3. **Show the change AND its location**: "We have added X (Results, p. 7, lines 142-150; new Table 2)." Quote the new text.
4. **Concede gracefully** when the reviewer is right — make the change and say so.
5. **Rebut with evidence, not attitude** when the reviewer is wrong or asks something infeasible: explain why, cite evidence, and offer the best alternative (e.g., add a limitation, a sensitivity analysis, or softened claim).
6. **Reconcile conflicting reviewers** openly; let the editor see your reasoning.

## Format
- A letter to the editor summarizing the main changes.
- Then, per reviewer: the reviewer's comment quoted in full → your response → the exact manuscript change (with location). Use a two-color or quoted/indented scheme so changes are obvious.
- Provide a clean and a tracked-changes manuscript if requested.

## Traps
- Claiming a change you didn't make (reviewers re-check). Partial answers. Defensiveness. Re-arguing the original submission instead of revising. Missing the editor's own requests.
