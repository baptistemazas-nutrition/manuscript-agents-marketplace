# Journal Selection

Pick the venue before formatting anything — it dictates structure, length, and style.

## 1. Fit (the first filter)
- **Scope & audience**: read the journal's aims; does your core claim match what they publish? Check 3-5 recent issues for similar work.
- **Article type**: original research / review / case report / methods / brief — your manuscript must match an accepted type.
- **Readership**: specialist vs. broad. A strong-but-narrow result may fit a specialist journal better than a rejected stretch at a general one.

## 2. Trade-offs to weigh
| Factor | Questions |
|---|---|
| Impact / prestige | Journal Impact Factor, CiteScore, h5-index, field rank (quartile) — useful but imperfect; fit and readership often matter more |
| Speed | Time to first decision, to acceptance, to publication (many journals publish these) |
| Open access | Subscription vs. gold/green OA; **APC cost**; does your funder mandate OA (Plan S)? Is there a fee waiver? |
| Acceptance odds | Desk-reject rate, scope tightness, novelty bar |
| Indexing | PubMed/MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, DOAJ — required for discoverability and some evaluations |

## 3. Predatory-journal screen (MANDATORY)
Reject venues showing these red flags:
- Spam invitations, flattery, guaranteed fast acceptance.
- APC hidden until after acceptance; no clear fee.
- Fake or unverifiable metrics ("index copernicus value"), fake impact factors.
- No real peer review, implausibly broad scope, editorial board unreachable or fabricated.
- Not in **DOAJ** (for OA), not indexed where it claims, mismatched ISSN.
- Use the "Think. Check. Submit." checklist; verify indexing directly at the index's site.

## 4. Tools
Scope-match finders (JANE, journal finders from publishers, Web of Science Manuscript Matcher) suggest candidates from your title/abstract — treat as leads, then apply the fit + predatory screen yourself.

## Output
A ranked shortlist (3-5) with, per journal: scope-match rationale, type fit, metric snapshot, OA/APC, estimated speed and odds, indexing, and a passed/failed predatory screen.
